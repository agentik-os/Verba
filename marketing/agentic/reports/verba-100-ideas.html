That is Verba: private on your Mac, and it acts only after you confirm. Here are 100 concrete features to reach that future first, each with why it matters, how it works, what it is for, and the advantage it buys.

Next-gen dictation and understanding

001
Live Rewind
Why Voice-first only wins if you never have to stop and fix it yourself. Most dictation tools freeze the transcript the moment you pause, so a false start or a wrong word choice becomes a manual cleanup chore, which quietly pushes people back to typing.
How Verba keeps a short rolling buffer of raw audio and draft text behind the visible cursor. Say a correction phrase like scratch that or I meant, and Verba rewinds the buffer, re-listens to what came before, and silently rewrites the last clause in place, no dialog box, no highlighted diff.
For You are dictating a client email, say the wrong client name, catch it mid sentence, and just correct yourself out loud instead of stopping to select and retype.
Edge Competitors treat correction as a separate edit mode. Verba treats it as part of speaking, so the text stays clean without your hands ever touching the keyboard. Private on-device AI means the rewind buffer never leaves your Mac.

002
Disfluency Filter with a Dial
Why Real speech is full of um, repeated words, and false starts. A one-size-fits-all filter either leaves them in (unprofessional output) or strips too much (loses your actual meaning, like a genuine repeated word for emphasis).
How A single visible slider from raw to polished. Verba's language model scores each disfluency by type (filler, repetition, false start, self correction) and a confidence that it changes meaning, then applies the dial threshold live as you speak, not as a batch pass afterward.
For Switching from a quick Slack message (raw, keep your voice) to a formal report (polished, remove every filler) without opening a settings menu each time.
Edge Rivals ship exactly one cleanup style baked in. A tunable, per-document dial with instant feedback is a control level none of them expose, and it runs entirely on your Mac so your speech patterns never get profiled off-device.
leapfrogeffort S
003
Code Voice Mode
Why Developers are one of the highest-value voice-first audiences, but dictation tools transcribe code speech as prose: they hear underscore as the word underscore, not an actual character, and mangle camelCase and brackets.
How A detected or manually toggled mode that maps spoken syntax (snake case, camel case, open paren, arrow function, dot) to literal characters and symbols, keeps identifiers un-autocorrected against dictionary words, and understands common language keyword vocabularies well enough to guess casing from context.
For Dictating a function name, a git commit message, or a code comment straight into your editor without switching to typing halfway through.
Edge No mainstream dictation tool treats code speech as a first-class mode. Owning developers as a beachhead audience, on-device and private, is a moat consumer dictation apps have no reason to build.

004
Whisper and Room Noise Lock-In
Why Voice-first has to work everywhere, not just in a quiet office. People dictate in open-plan offices, on trains, and late at night when they can only whisper, and that is exactly where today's dictation quality collapses.
How An acoustic pre-pass classifies the input as whisper, low SNR, or normal speech and switches to a matched decoding profile, paired with a short one-time enrollment where Verba learns your specific whisper acoustics and typical background noise print for your usual rooms.
For Dictating a sensitive note in a shared office at 6am without waking anyone, or drafting in a noisy cafe without repeating yourself three times.
Edge Most tools quietly degrade in these exact conditions and never tell you why. Verba both detects the mode and adapts to it, delivering usable text where competitors deliver garbage, entirely processed privately on the Mac.

Speaker-Aware Meeting Capture
Why Long-form dictation is not just one person talking, it is often you narrating a meeting or reviewing a recording. Flat transcripts with no sense of who said what force manual cleanup that erases most of the time saved by voice.
How On-device speaker diarization tags turns by voice print (not identity, just consistent labels like Speaker 1, Speaker 2), and Verba lets you say assign that to Sarah once, after which JARVIS can act on labeled turns, like turning a specific person's action items into tasks after you confirm.
For Dictating notes during or after a call with two or three people, and getting a transcript that already separates who committed to what.
Edge Consumer dictation tools assume one speaker. Combining diarization with JARVIS's confirm-before-act model turns a meeting transcript into structured follow ups, a workflow rivals do not connect end to end, all without audio ever leaving the Mac.

006
Intent Punctuation Engine
Why Punctuation from dictation today is either dictated aloud (clunky, breaks flow) or auto-inserted from pauses (wrong half the time, since pause length does not reliably signal a period versus a comma versus a question).
How A prosody and syntax model reads pitch contour, tempo, and sentence structure together, not pause length alone, to decide period, comma, question mark, or em dash-free parenthetical, and it learns your personal speaking rhythm over time so short-pause-but-new-thought patterns get punctuated correctly for you specifically.
For Dictating a long, structured email with several sub-clauses and having it come out correctly punctuated without you ever saying the word comma.
Edge Most tools use a generic pause threshold that is wrong for fast or rhythmic talkers. A model that adapts to your personal cadence, on-device, gets measurably fewer punctuation errors over time in a way a shared, one-size-fits-all model tuned to an average speaker cannot match.

Mid-Sentence Language Switch
Why Multilingual speakers naturally mix languages inside one sentence (a French phrase in an English email, a technical English term in a Spanish note), and single-language-locked dictation either mangles the foreign phrase or forces an awkward manual language toggle.
How A streaming language identifier runs per phrase, not per session, switching the active recognition model mid-utterance without a restart, and a personal glossary lets you pin specific words or names to always render in one language regardless of surrounding context.
For Writing a bilingual email, dropping in a foreign phrase or a person's name in its native spelling, and continuing in your main language without stopping dictation.
Edge Most tools force a single locked language per session. Real-time per-phrase switching plus a pinned glossary is a materially better experience for the large multilingual population competitors treat as an edge case.

Long-Form Structure Sense
Why Dictating a long document (a report, a proposal, a blog draft) out loud tends to produce a wall of run-on text, because spoken language does not naturally signal paragraph breaks, headings, or lists the way typing with visual feedback does.
How Verba tracks topic shifts and structural cues (new paragraph, first point, in summary) across the whole session, not just the current sentence, and proposes paragraph breaks, headings, and numbered lists as you go, shown as light in-line suggestions you can accept with a word or ignore by continuing to talk.
For Dictating a full first draft of a report end to end and getting back something that already reads as a structured document instead of one giant paragraph you have to break up by hand.
Edge Dictation tools optimize for short utterances; long-form structure is largely ignored. Owning the long-form use case is a clear product gap, and doing it with visible, confirmable suggestions fits Verba's confirm-before-change ethos.

Correction Memory
Why Dictation tools make you fix the same mistake over and over (a misheard name, a wrong homophone, a specific technical term) because they have no memory between sessions. That repeated friction is what quietly kills trust in voice as a primary input.
How Every manual correction you make (typed or spoken) is logged locally with the original misheard text and your fix, and a lightweight on-device personalization layer folds these corrections back into recognition weighting, so a name you fixed once is heard right the next ten times.
For You correct your unusual last name or a niche product name once, and every dictation session afterward gets it right without you repeating the correction.
Edge Most tools treat every session as a blank slate. Persistent, private, on-device personalization that compounds over weeks of use is an edge that gets stronger the longer someone uses Verba, which is a retention moat no first-run, memoryless model offers.

Confirmed Voice Macros
Why The next step past clean dictation is turning a recurring spoken pattern into a repeatable action, which is exactly where Verba's private-plus-acts-plus-confirm position has no rival: competitors either just transcribe or auto-act with no visible checkpoint.
How Verba notices when you dictate the same structured pattern repeatedly (a standup update, a status email, a specific note format) and offers to save it as a named voice macro. For Saying daily standup and having Verba draft your usual three-part update format, then confirming before JARVIS posts or sends it.
Edge No competitor combines transcription quality with an act-only-after-confirm execution layer, so this feature is only buildable on Verba's existing trust model. It is the clearest expression of the thesis that voice-first must also be safe-first.

Why The future of voice is not one command, it is a spoken sequence of intent, the way you'd brief a real assistant in one breath.
How JARVIS parses a single spoken request into an ordered plan of app-level steps (open, fill, send, file, schedule), shows the whole plan as a readable preview before touching anything, then executes step by step with one confirm for the whole chain.
For Say once: draft the invoice from this call's notes, save it to the client folder, and put a follow-up on the calendar for Friday. JARVIS builds the three-step plan, you confirm once, it runs all three.
Edge Rivals force one command per turn or hand the whole session to a server agent with no preview. Verba lets you brief like a human and see the full plan before anything happens, on your Mac.
