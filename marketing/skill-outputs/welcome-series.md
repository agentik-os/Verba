# Welcome Series: Verba

**Business model:** Productivity SaaS (Mac app, free download with a subscription unlock), closest to the B2B SaaS arc: activation, use case, pricing clarity, trial nudge.

**Hero USP:** A private Mac voice agent. It writes clean text in any app and, as JARVIS, plans an action and waits for your confirm before it touches anything.

**Body:**

Hey, welcome to Verba.

Here's the whole idea: you talk, Verba writes it down clean, in whatever app you're already in. No copy, no paste, no switching windows.

**Do this first:** open Mail, Slack, or Notes, hit your Verba shortcut, and say one full sentence out loud. Watch it land on the page, cleaned up and ready to send.

- You can talk for a full sentence or a full hour. Verba keeps up either way.

## Email 3: Day 4

**Goal:** Teach the mode system so the user understands the product's depth and picks up a second habit beyond raw dictation.

**Subject line:** The six ways Verba can write for you
**Alt subject A/B:** Raw, Polish, Intent, Coding: pick your mode
**Preheader:** Each mode does one job well. Here's when to reach for which one.

**Body:**

Verba isn't one dictation mode, it's six, and each one solves a different moment.

**Raw.** Exactly what you said, transcribed. Fastest, no cleanup. Good for notes to yourself.

**Polish.** Cleans up your filler words and false starts into a tight, readable sentence. Good for Slack and Mail.

**Intent.** Understands what you're trying to say, not just the words, and writes it properly even if you rambled. Good for anything you'd normally rewrite twice.

**Coding.** Turns a spoken, messy idea into a clear prompt for your editor. Good for Cursor, VS Code, or your terminal-based coding tool.

**Context.** Reads what's already on your screen so it can reply to the actual email or message in front of you, not a blank page.

**Translate.** Speak in your language, Verba writes in the one you choose. No manual switching.

**One line to keep:** match the mode to the moment, not the other way around. Most people land on two or three favorites within the first week.

**CTA:** [Switch modes and try one you haven't used] (button)

Gareth

P.S. Context mode is the one people underestimate the most. Try replying to a real email on your screen with it once, and it clicks.

## Email 4: Day 6

**Goal:** Address the three real objections that stop upgrade or continued use: privacy, whether JARVIS can be trusted to act, and what the plans actually include.

**Subject line:** The questions everyone asks in week one
**Alt subject A/B:** Is it private? Can JARVIS be trusted? Answered
**Preheader:** Three honest answers, no marketing spin, before you decide to go further.

**Body:**

Three questions land in our inbox every week. Here they are, answered straight.

**"Where does my voice actually go?"**
Nowhere it doesn't need to. Your dictation is processed on your Mac. It is not a transcript sitting on a server somewhere waiting to be read.

**"What happens if JARVIS gets it wrong?"**
It can't act without you. JARVIS plans the steps, shows you exactly what it's about to do, asks when something is ambiguous, and only executes after you hit confirm. Cancel is always one tap away.

**"What's actually in the free version versus paid?"**
Raw dictation is free, forever, no card required. Every other mode, Polish, Intent, Coding, Context, Translate, plus JARVIS and the connected apps, is part of the paid plan ([to confirm] pricing, shown in-app and on verba.run/pricing).

If something else is holding you back, just reply to this email. A real person reads it.

**CTA:** [See what's included in each plan] (button, links to pricing)

Gareth

P.S. The confirm-before-acting step is not a limitation we're apologizing for. It's the whole design. Verba should never surprise you.

## Setup notes

- **Trigger:** New account created after downloading Verba (post-signup, Clerk auth completes). Fires immediately on account creation, not on first dictation.
- **Exit conditions:** Subscriber upgrades to a paid plan at any point → exit remaining sequence, move to a paid-plan onboarding flow instead. Subscriber uninstalls or requests deletion → exit immediately.
- **Suggested send window:** SaaS/productivity audience, weekday-skewed. Send Email 1 immediately on signup regardless of time (it's transactional in feel). For Emails 2 to 5, send Tue to Thu, 9 to 10am recipient-local, avoiding Monday inbox pileup and Friday afternoon drop-off.
- **A/B test idea (first 30 days):** Test the Email 1 subject line, "You're in. Let's get your first word down" versus "Welcome to Verba. One key, that's it", against first-dictation completion rate within 24 hours, not just open rate. That's the real activation event this series is built to drive.
